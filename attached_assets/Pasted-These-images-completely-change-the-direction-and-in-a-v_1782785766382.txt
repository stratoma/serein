These images completely change the direction—and in a very good way.

What you've created isn't simply a skincare website. It feels closer to an editorial home, a design journal, or a gallery. The photography carries so much narrative that the copy's role should shift from *telling* to *guiding*.

My biggest recommendation is this:

> **The copy should feel like museum wall text, not marketing copy.**

Every sentence should create space for the imagery instead of competing with it.

Below is what I would give Replit.

---

# SEREIN Website Direction

## Overall Experience

Do **not** build this like a traditional ecommerce website.

Do **not** prioritize selling above atmosphere.

Instead, create an experience that feels somewhere between:

* a beautifully designed editorial magazine
* a contemporary art gallery
* a boutique hotel website
* a luxury fragrance house
* a slow design journal

The customer should feel immersed before they ever feel sold to.

The photography is the primary storyteller.

The copy exists to create rhythm.

Silence is part of the design.

---

# Emotional Direction

The site should feel:

* approachable but composed
* luxurious without exclusivity
* warm rather than polished
* tactile rather than glossy
* intelligent without being academic
* modern African rather than "African-inspired"
* slow
* grounded
* architectural
* sensory

Every page should invite exploration, never urgency.

---

# Copy Philosophy

Assume the customer is already interested.

Never persuade aggressively.

Never oversell.

Never explain what the image already communicates.

Instead:

* provide context
* deepen emotion
* introduce ritual
* leave room for interpretation

If an image already communicates warmth, don't write:

> Warm. Cozy. Luxurious.

Instead write:

> A scent that settles long after the room has quieted.

---

# Writing Style

Write in short paragraphs.

Use generous white space.

Allow breathing room between thoughts.

Sentences should feel composed.

Example:

---

Root.

Fresh mint.
Earth.
Renewal.

A scent that returns you
to yourself.

---

Instead of

> Mint + Moss combines fresh mint, cedarwood, green tea and amber musk to create a sophisticated spa-like fragrance perfect for relaxation.

Never write like that.

---

# Headlines

Headlines should rarely exceed four words.

Examples

Transform.

Root.

Become.

The Collection

Quiet Rituals

Made Slowly

Daily Return

The Studio

Materials

The Morrow Trio

Nothing should feel like marketing.

---

# Body Copy

Paragraphs should stay between

20–60 words.

Never walls of text.

Every paragraph should feel intentional.

---

# Scrolling Experience

Scrolling should feel cinematic.

Large image.

Small paragraph.

Whitespace.

Fade.

Next image.

Quote.

Product.

Pause.

Repeat.

The pace should slow the visitor down.

---

# Typography

Typography should feel editorial.

Elegant serif headlines.

Simple readable body text.

Large spacing.

Avoid heavy bold text.

Avoid shouting.

Everything should feel quiet.

---

# Use of Photography

Photography should dominate.

Images should often occupy:

70–90% of a section.

Text should never cover important details.

Instead, let text live beside images or float in negative space.

Every image should be allowed to breathe.

---

# Image Captions

Treat images almost like gallery pieces.

Examples

Thoughtfully sourced.

Handcrafted in small batches.

The ritual begins long before the jar is opened.

Rich botanicals.

Light through linen.

Quiet mornings.

Never over-caption.

---

# Motion

Motion should feel almost invisible.

Slow fades.

Soft image reveals.

Parallax no more than 5–10%.

Buttons gently brighten.

No bouncing.

No flashy animations.

Everything should move like sunlight.

---

# User Experience

Every interaction should communicate care.

Hover states should feel gentle.

Transitions should feel soft.

Whitespace should increase confidence.

The customer should never feel rushed.

---

# Overall Impression

When someone closes the website they should feel:

> "That was beautiful."

before they think

> "I should buy that."

Buying should become the natural conclusion of experiencing the brand.

---

## Most Important Observation

Looking through your campaign, one thing stands out more than anything else.

The photography doesn't sell body butter.

It sells **a way of living**.

The recurring themes are:

* natural light
* architecture
* stone
* linen
* botanical studies
* African modernism
* books
* ritual objects
* quiet interiors
* sculptural composition
* contemplative portraiture

That means SEREIN is not just a body care brand. It is presenting an aesthetic philosophy. The website should reflect that by feeling like an editorial experience first and a storefront second.

If Replit can preserve that restraint—allowing imagery to lead, copy to support, and generous whitespace to set the pace—the site will feel distinctive rather than resembling another premium skincare template.
